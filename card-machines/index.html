Terminal categories

Six types of card machine, and who each one suits.

We start with categories rather than models. Once we know which fits, we compare the specific devices and providers available to you.

Countertop

Wired to power and network at a fixed till.

Best for: Shops, reception desks, takeaways, salons with a front desk.

  • Most reliable connection (ethernet or broadband)
  • Prints receipts on the spot
  • Lowest-maintenance option

Portable

Wireless over Wi-Fi or Bluetooth, anywhere within your premises.

Best for: Restaurants, pubs, cafés and larger retail floors.

  • Take the terminal to the customer
  • Pairs with a base station or your Wi-Fi
  • Good battery for a full service

Mobile & 4G

Built-in SIM with mobile data, so it works wherever there’s signal.

Best for: Market traders, deliveries, events, taxis and on-site trades.

  • No Wi-Fi or phone line needed
  • Wi-Fi fallback on many models
  • Offline mode for patchy signal

Unattended

Ruggedised, tamper-resistant readers built into machines and kiosks.

Best for: Vending, parking, EV charging, laundrettes and self-service.

  • Contactless-only or full Chip & PIN
  • Built to run 24/7
  • Remote monitoring and reporting

EPOS-integrated

Connected to your till so the amount is sent to the terminal automatically.

Best for: Busy retail and hospitality with an existing EPOS system.

  • No double-keying or mistyped amounts
  • Sales and payments reconcile together
  • Faster checkout at peak times

Pay-at-table & tipping

Split bills, add gratuities and take payment at the table.

Best for: Restaurants, bars, hotels and hospitality groups.

  • Split by item or equal shares
  • Preset or custom tip amounts
  • Tip reporting for staff payouts

Before you buy

Six things to settle first.

Get these right and the terminal will suit you for years. Get them wrong and you’ll be fighting it every busy Saturday.

  1. Connectivity

    Fixed line, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth or 4G — chosen on where you trade and how reliable the signal is there.

  2. Battery & durability

    Matters if the terminal leaves the counter, especially through a long or busy day.

  3. Tipping & gratuities

    How tips are added, split between staff and shown in reporting.

  4. Refunds & voids

    How easily staff can process them, and whether a supervisor PIN is needed.

  5. Offline mode

    Storing transactions when the network drops, then sending them when it’s back.

  6. Integration

    Standalone is simpler; integrated suits larger or higher-volume operations.

Rent, buy or bundle?

How you pay for the hardware matters too.

Terminal costs are often where a “cheap” deal becomes expensive. We lay out the options side by side so you can choose with the full picture.

Rent

Monthly rental

A fixed monthly fee per terminal, usually including support and replacements.

  • Low upfront cost
  • Swaps if a device fails

Watch for: rental terms that run longer than your processing contract and are hard to exit.

Buy

Outright purchase

Pay once for the device and own it.

  • No ongoing hardware fee
  • Often easier to switch provider later

Watch for: devices that are locked to one provider and can’t move with you.

Bundle

Included in pricing

Hardware folded into your transaction pricing or a monthly plan.

  • Simple single bill
  • Can suit lower volumes

Watch for: minimum monthly charges and higher rates that cover the hardware.
