The process, plainly

Four steps, and honest timelines.

No jargon and no promises we can’t keep. Here’s exactly what happens when you work with DPayments, and a straight answer on timing.

Get a quote →

Understand the business

We start with how you trade — not with a product.

  • How you take payments: in person, online, by phone or a mix
  • Your typical card mix (debit, credit, Amex, business, international)
  • Average transaction size and monthly turnover
  • Your premises, staff and the software you already use
  • Where you’re growing over the next year or two

Nothing is recommended until we understand what you actually need.

Compare the market

We weigh acquirers, gateways and terminals against your profile.

  • Transaction pricing: blended vs interchange-plus
  • Hardware: rent, buy or bundled
  • Contract length, auto-renewal and notice periods
  • Minimum monthly charges, PCI fees and exit fees
  • Settlement speed and any reserves

You get a recommendation with the reasoning shown — not a single take-it-or-leave-it quote.

Set up and onboard

We handle the parts that usually slow businesses down.

  • Application and Know-Your-Customer (KYC) checks
  • Terminal configuration, receipts, tipping and refund settings
  • Online checkout, payment links or virtual terminal setup
  • EPOS or platform integration
  • PCI DSS self-assessment guidance

The goal is for going live to be the easy part.

Stay on it

Payments aren’t set-and-forget. We keep watching.

  • Periodic check-ins on pricing and statements
  • Help with new sites, extra terminals or new channels
  • Support with chargebacks and disputes
  • A heads-up before contract renewal dates
  • We’ll tell you if a better option appears

You have someone on your side long after the paperwork is done.

How long does it take?

A straight answer: it depends on underwriting.

We won’t promise “live in 24 hours”. Setup is quick for a straightforward business with documents ready, and takes longer when more due diligence is needed. We’ll give you a realistic timeframe before you commit.

The biggest factor in your control is having your documents ready. Here’s what providers typically ask for.

Identity

Photo ID and proof of address for directors, owners and anyone with significant control.

Business details

Company number or sole-trader details, trading address, what you sell and how.

Bank account

The business account where settlements will be paid, plus recent bank statements.

Processing history

If you already take cards, recent merchant statements help underwriting move faster.

Website

For online payments: a live site with terms, refund policy, privacy policy and contact details.

Expected volumes

Estimated monthly card turnover and average transaction value.
