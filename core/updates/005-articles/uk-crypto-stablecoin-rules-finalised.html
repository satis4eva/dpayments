The UK now has a finished rulebook for crypto. On 30 June 2026 the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) published its final policy statements for the new cryptoasset regime, after three years of discussion and consultation papers. The package covers admissions and disclosures, market abuse, stablecoin issuance, regulated crypto activities and a dedicated prudential (capital) regime.

For businesses that accept crypto, hold it or build products on top of it, the important part is no longer whether the UK will regulate the market, but when and who needs permission.

The key dates

  • 30 September 2026: the FCA authorisation gateway opened for firms that want to carry on the new regulated crypto activities.
  • 28 February 2027: the application window closes.
  • 25 October 2027: the regime comes into force. From then on, in-scope firms need FCA authorisation to operate.

Who needs to be authorised?

Crypto trading platforms, custodians, stablecoin issuers and staking providers are all expected to need FCA authorisation before the regime takes effect. Firms must meet financial-resilience requirements, including capital and stress testing, as well as new market-integrity rules aimed at insider dealing and market manipulation.

The FCA said it simplified parts of the regime after industry feedback, including lighter capital requirements for stablecoin issuers than first proposed.

What the stablecoin rules cover

The stablecoin policy statement (PS26/10) sets the final rules for UK-issued stablecoins: what assets must back them, how those assets are segregated and safeguarded, record-keeping and reconciliation, and how and when holders can redeem their tokens. Stablecoins that become systemic will fall under a separate regime run jointly with the Bank of England.

Importantly for international businesses, stablecoins issued outside the UK can still circulate here if they meet the FCA's standards.

What this means if you accept crypto payments

Most merchants don't issue tokens or run exchanges. They use a payment gateway that accepts crypto from customers and settles to the business. Under the new regime, the firms that provide those services, including custody, conversion and settlement, are the ones that will need to be authorised. That makes partner choice more important than ever:

  • Check your provider's plans. Ask whether they intend to apply for UK authorisation, and on what timetable.
  • Understand who holds the funds. Know whether your provider holds client crypto, and how it is safeguarded.
  • Look at stablecoin choices. If you settle in stablecoins, check how the token you use is backed and redeemed.
  • Plan for the transition. Make sure your provider can keep serving you through October 2027.
