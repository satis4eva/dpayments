On 2 June 2026, the UK switched on its first new payment scheme since Faster Payments launched in 2008. It's called commercial Variable Recurring Payments (commercial VRP or cVRP), and it could change how businesses collect regular payments.

What is a Variable Recurring Payment?

A VRP is an open banking payment. The customer authorises a business, through a regulated provider, to take payments directly from their bank account on a repeating basis, within limits the customer sets, such as a maximum per payment or per month. The customer can see and cancel the arrangement in their banking app.

VRPs already existed in a limited form called "sweeping", which moves money between a customer's own accounts. The new scheme extends them to paying businesses.

Who runs it?

The scheme is operated by the UK Payments Initiative (UKPI), an industry-owned company incorporated in December 2025 and backed by 31 founding firms, including the largest UK retail banks and a range of payment providers. UKPI sets the shared rulebook, commercial model and operational standards. The FCA welcomed the launch as a major step for open banking and said it would consult on a regulatory framework by the end of 2026.

Who can use it today?

The rollout is happening in waves:

  • Wave 1 (live now): regulated and lower-risk sectors such as financial services, government, utilities and rail. At launch, participating banks covered around 75% of UK current accounts.
  • Wave 2 (next): general e-commerce and subscriptions, with industry work under way on how consumers will be protected when they buy this way.

VRP vs Direct Debit vs card-on-file

  • Direct Debit is familiar and cheap but slow to set up and settle, and failed collections are a growing cost. One provider reported Direct Debit failures 9% higher in 2026 than in 2025.
  • Card-on-file is quick to set up but carries card fees, card expiries and chargebacks.
  • Commercial VRP combines account-to-account payments with flexible amounts and customer-set limits, aiming for lower processing costs and faster settlement.

Should your business offer it?

If you're in a Wave 1 sector and collect variable amounts, such as utility bills, loan repayments or usage-based charges, it's worth adding VRP alongside your existing options now, since bank coverage is already high. Everyone else should get ready for Wave 2. Many businesses will run VRP alongside Direct Debit and cards for some time.
