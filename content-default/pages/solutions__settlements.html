What we look at

Settlement terms matter as much as rates.

Two providers with the same rate can leave you with very different cash flow.

Settlement currencies

Be paid in GBP, EUR, USD and other major currencies into matching accounts, to avoid unnecessary conversions.

Payout timing

T+1, T+2 or T+3 — and whether faster options are available once you have a trading history.

Settlement frequency

Daily, weekly or on demand, depending on what suits your cash flow.

Reserves

Whether a rolling or fixed reserve applies, how much, for how long, and when it’s released.

Gross vs net

Whether fees are deducted before payout or invoiced separately — it affects how you reconcile.

Reporting

Settlement reports that tie each payout back to the transactions in it.

Stablecoin settlements

Faster cross-border settlement, through partners.

For businesses with international flows, some partners can settle your card takings in regulated stablecoins such as USDC, alongside or instead of fiat. That can mean faster access to funds across borders and outside banking hours.

We act as an introducer only: settlement is provided by the partner, and we never hold your funds. Stablecoins carry their own risks, and whether this is suitable depends on your business, your jurisdiction and your accounting setup.

What does T+1 mean?

Funds reach your account one business day after the transaction date. T+2 is two days, and so on.

Why would a provider hold a reserve?

To cover potential refunds and chargebacks. It’s more common for new businesses, online sellers and higher-risk sectors. It should always be disclosed before you sign.

Does DPayments hold my money?

No. Settlement is always made directly to you by the acquirer or partner. We don’t hold or touch merchant funds.

DPayments is an independent payments partner. We arrange these services through regulated acquirers, payment service providers and specialist partners; availability, pricing and approval depend on your business, sector and the partner’s underwriting.
