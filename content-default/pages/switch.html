Anatomy of a statement

Every line, in plain English.

Once each charge has a name, it’s much easier to judge whether it’s fair. Here’s how the typical lines split between genuine cost and provider margin.

  1. Merchant service charge Margin + cost

    The headline percentage on your card turnover. It’s the figure most often quoted, but on its own it says very little about what you really pay.

  2. Interchange Cost

    A wholesale fee set by the card networks and paid to your customer’s bank. No provider can waive it. Transparent statements show it separately; blended ones hide it.

  3. Scheme fees Cost

    Per-transaction fees paid to Visa and Mastercard. Small individually, significant in total, and a pass-through cost rather than provider margin.

  4. Acquirer margin Margin

    What your provider keeps. Interchange-plus pricing shows it as a fixed figure; blended pricing folds it into the headline rate.

  5. Authorisation fees Margin

    A small charge on every authorisation request — including the smallest sales, which is where it hurts most.

  6. PCI fees Margin

    Charges for data-security compliance, plus a monthly non-compliance fee if your annual self-assessment lapses.

  7. Minimum monthly service charge Margin

    A monthly floor charged regardless of turnover, so quiet months can cost more than the rate suggests.

  8. Terminal rental Margin

    A separate, often longer agreement that can outlast everything else on your statement and wipe out the savings of a cheap processing deal.

Where savings usually come from

It’s rarely just the rate.

When merchants are overpaying, it’s usually down to a few familiar causes. These are the first things we look for.

Pricing model

Moving from blended to interchange-plus pricing can cut costs significantly if most of your customers pay with UK consumer debit cards.

Fees you don’t need

PCI non-compliance fees, duplicate service charges, statement fees and minimums that no longer match your turnover.

Hardware costs

Paying rental on more terminals than you use, or on devices you’ve long since paid for several times over.

Card mix

Premium, commercial and international cards cost more. Pricing that reflects your real mix can make a real difference.

Outgrown pricing

Rates agreed when you were smaller. As turnover grows, your pricing should improve too — it often doesn’t unless you ask.

Contract timing

Knowing when your term and notice window end, so you can switch without exit fees — or negotiate with your current provider.
