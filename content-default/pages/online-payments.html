Ways to accept online

Pick one, or combine them.

Most businesses use two or three of these together. We’ll recommend the mix that fits how your customers actually buy.

Hosted checkout

A secure payment page hosted by the provider and connected to your website. Card data never reaches your servers, which keeps your PCI obligations to a minimum.

  • Branded with your logo and colours
  • Cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay
  • Works on mobile and desktop
  • 3D Secure 2 built in

Good for: Online shops, booking sites and any business selling through its own website.

Payment links

A one-off secure payment page you send by text, email or messaging app. No website needed — the customer taps the link and pays.

  • Create a link in seconds
  • Set an amount, reference and expiry
  • See when it’s been paid
  • Great for follow-ups after a call

Good for: Invoices, deposits, bookings, quotes and social-media sales.

Virtual terminal (MOTO)

A secure web page where you or your team key in card details given over the phone or by post. For businesses that sell through conversation.

  • Runs in any browser — no hardware
  • Multiple staff logins
  • Refunds and receipts from the same screen
  • Option to send a link instead

Good for: Trades, travel, professional services and anyone taking telephone orders.

Recurring & subscriptions

Securely stored card details billed on a schedule you set, with authentication and automatic retries on failed payments handled for you.

  • Weekly, monthly or custom schedules
  • Fixed or variable amounts
  • Card updater to reduce failed payments
  • Customer notifications

Good for: Memberships, gyms, retainers, subscription boxes and instalment plans.

Platform integrations

Ready-made plugins and connections for the major ecommerce, booking and invoicing platforms, plus developer APIs for custom builds.

  • Popular shop and website builders
  • Invoicing and accounting tools
  • Booking and scheduling software
  • API and developer docs for custom sites

Good for: Businesses already using a platform who want payments to just work.

One reconciliation

In-person and online takings reported together, so you see the whole picture and month-end is simpler.

  • One dashboard for every channel
  • Exports for your accountant
  • Settlement reports by day
  • Fewer spreadsheets

Good for: Any business taking payments in more than one way.

The parts worth understanding

Three things every online seller should know.

3D Secure 2

The authentication step for online card payments, where a customer may confirm through their banking app. It’s required for most UK and EU online payments under Strong Customer Authentication rules, and it shifts fraud liability on authenticated transactions to the card issuer. A good setup only challenges risky payments, so most customers sail through.

Chargebacks

When a cardholder disputes a payment, the amount is reversed while it’s investigated. Receipts, proof of delivery and your communications help you win. Clear refund policies, accurate descriptions and fraud checks help prevent disputes in the first place — and that’s always cheaper.

Settlement timing

Authorisation takes about a second, but settlement usually takes one to three business days after the sale, depending on the acquirer, the card scheme and your terms. New accounts may start on slower settlement or a reserve while a trading history builds up.

Getting approved

What providers look for on your website.

Underwriters review your site before approving online payments. Having these in place up front avoids delays — we’ll check them with you before applying.

  1. Clear products and prices

    What you sell, what it costs and in which currency.

  2. Terms & conditions

    Including how and when orders are fulfilled.

  3. Refund and returns policy

    Easy to find, and consistent with consumer law.

  4. Privacy policy

    How you handle customer data under UK GDPR.

  5. Contact details

    A business address, email and ideally a phone number.

  6. Secure site (HTTPS)

    A valid SSL certificate on every page, especially checkout.
